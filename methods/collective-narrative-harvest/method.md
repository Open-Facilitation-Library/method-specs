# Collective Narrative Methodology harvest

Use this protocol when people want a recognizable account of their conversation for an agreed audience, including readers who missed the event. A World Café can supply the conversation. Its convening design belongs upstream. Participatory Narrative Inquiry and its participant interpretation of personal stories have their own OFL specification effort (#47).

The host announces the terms before convening. Contributors supply group reports and individual additions. A named human steward holds the source record, edits the account and answers corrections. These are six phases of one facilitated harvest. The reference runtime field identifies the format target only. All handoffs can use paper or access-controlled documents.

## Stage: agree-terms

**Goal** — Agree how contributions will be used.

**Host.** Before the conversation, explain who will read the narrative, how reports will be captured, who can see originals, how contributors can choose credit or anonymity, and who will answer corrections. Ask everyone to identify a feasible way to contribute independently of a table host. Announce the closing date, time zone, accessible alternatives and correction window. EFL describes a same-day midnight deadline. Set and communicate a workable local equivalent when adapting it. If recording or tool processing lacks permission, arrange an agreed written channel.

**Output** — announced harvest terms.

## Stage: collect-reports

**Goal** — Hear the conversation in context.

**Host and contributors.** Ask each group, “What would someone who missed your conversation need to hear to understand what came up and why?” Record the question, group, round and report. Invite people to add what was missed or described differently. Accept those individual additions through the announced channel until it closes. A host report describes what the host heard. Give others an opportunity to speak for themselves.

**Output** — reports and additions.

## Stage: assemble-sources

**Goal** — Close and check the source material.

**Steward.** At cutoff, assemble the dated reports and eligible additions. Keep the original text or permitted recording. Transcribe accurately and give each passage a durable pointer such as report, round and timestamp. Check uncertain words against the original or ask the contributor. Mark any remaining uncertainty. Keep later reflections and previously written or third-party resources separately identified. Limit access to the agreed readers for each record.

**Output** — eligible source record.

## Stage: cluster-passages

**Goal** — Cluster passages across rounds.

**Steward.** Group intact passages by what they discuss, retaining their source pointers. Read surrounding material before moving a passage. Order clusters so an absent reader can follow the discussion. Preserve a lone objection beside the claim it qualifies. If the same story travelled to another table, retain the recurrence and its route where known. Count independent supporters only if the sources establish them.

**Output** — ordered passages with pointers.

## Stage: compose-narrative

**Goal** — Write and revisit the collective narrative.

**Steward.** Write in a collective voice where the material supports it. Use “some of us” or “one of us” to preserve a difference or a sole contribution, respecting the credit agreement. Keep meaningful repetition. Step away and return for an editorial pass. For each substantive sentence, identify its source and test its attribution, meaning and implied causal or decision claims. Read the sources in reverse to find eligible insights missing from the draft. Restore omissions and revise unsupported wording before returning it.

**Output** — checked narrative draft.

## Stage: return-and-correct

**Goal** — Return the account and steward corrections.

**Steward and contributors.** Give the agreed readers the narrative with event context, credits, version and correction contact. Ask, “Where does this misrepresent or leave out something you contributed?” Check each correction against its source. Restore omissions, repair misleading attribution and keep new reflections separately dated. Document unresolved objections and the responsible human decision. Announce who may reopen a version for a material error and how readers receive the correction. Silence alone supplies no evidence of endorsement.

**Output** — versioned narrative and correction record.

## Worked example

In a fictional discussion about opening hours, group A reports that evening opening would let shift workers come after work (A1). Group B reports that the last bus leaves at six and some people could be stranded (B1). An individual adds before cutoff that Saturday service runs later (I1).

A passage for the agreed audience could read, “Some of us saw a benefit to opening after shifts. Others raised a difficulty with getting home because the last bus leaves at six. One of us added that the bus runs later on Saturdays.”

The steward can trace the sentences to A1, B1 and I1 respectively. Each source also has a place in the passage. If the text says the group chose Saturday, remove the decision claim. If a contributor flags that the passage omitted the risk of being stranded, check B1 and restore that specific consequence. A bus timetable submitted after cutoff belongs with later resources, subject to its own reuse terms.

## Tool assistance and responsibility

With permission for the material, a tool may suggest transcriptions, clusters, alternative sequences or sentences needing source checks. The steward verifies each suggestion against the originals, chooses the narrative voice, handles objections and authorizes release to the agreed readers. Keep internal source pointers private wherever disclosure would identify an anonymous speaker.
