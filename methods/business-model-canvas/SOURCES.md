## Scope within the source tradition

The Canvas models a business's value creation, delivery, and capture. The surrounding Strategyzer toolkit also contains other tools and practices for detailed customer inquiry, experimentation, portfolios, and business-model design. A BMC session is not coverage of that entire toolkit. In particular, this spec does not import the Value Proposition Canvas, official experiment cards, a paid workshop agenda, or a certification standard. The final investigation record is an original OFL handoff, not a reproduction of those assets.

## Adaptation and evaluation status

The three-round chain groups six working checkpoints. This grouping, host progression checks, individual memory, role slugs, evidence labels, unit checks, accepted-ownership convention, privacy arrangements, and testing-handoff fields are OFL choices. They should not be attributed to Strategyzer as a mandatory workshop protocol. Contributors may move between blocks as the model develops. The nine blocks and their dependencies remain recognizable throughout. These are revisions of the initial unpublished draft; no previous version was released.

### Native artifact acceptance requirements

The requested Harmonica artifact is a rendered Canvas backed by a structured record, with the standard block arrangement and visible source credit. A runtime implementation must demonstrate all of the following; these are requirements, not recorded test results:

- All nine blocks appear in the appropriate arrangement, including explicitly unknown blocks; entries and cross-block relationships can be inspected at readable sizes.
- The displayed model identity and revision match the record supplied to the next round. Earlier proposals, disputed claims, alternative models, and confirmed corrections retain their distinct status.
- One evolving artifact belongs to each chain instance. Summary completion after each round initiates its next revision: initial draft before round two, reviewed update before round three, and closing revision after round three. Extraction or review failure remains distinguishable from summary readiness. Multiple chains in one workspace retain separate artifacts.
- Contributors can request corrections, responsible humans can confirm changes, and subsequent extraction does not silently overwrite confirmed corrections. Missing or stale artifacts remain visible and prevent the host from treating a dependent round as ready.
- Source inspection and artifact sharing respect existing access rights. Rendered or exported copies retain attribution and license/adaptation notices as applicable.

When a round opens, contributors and the facilitator must be able to identify the canvas revision they are reviewing. If a correction produces a new revision, retain the earlier revision so readers can tell which version a comment addressed.

If an entry comes from a group summary, cite that summary. Attribute an entry to an individual contribution only when its origin can be verified. Show the underlying contribution only to readers who already have access.

The YAML files under `evals/` are **illustrative, unexecuted blueprints** with synthetic inputs. They propose stage-local response checks grounded in the distinctions above. They have no model outputs, practitioner labels, calibrated judge, pass rate, or claim to production coverage. They cannot establish shared-artifact access, actual group correction, sound financial evidence, customer demand, or participant benefit. There is no new evaluator, conformance profile, runtime installer, or paid testing route in this change.
