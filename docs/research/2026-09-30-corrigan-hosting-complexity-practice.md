## What changes the question before a method is run

- **A gathering is a temporary intervention, not a representative miniature of its system.** The first letter says a powerful conversation does not establish structural change. The companion makes the distinctions explicit: people from a system are not *the* system; diverse perspectives do not by themselves represent it; insight in the room is not system-wide collective intelligence; changed relationships are not changed structures. For a method registry, faithful execution answers a narrower question than whether this was the right intervention or whether its results affected the context afterwards.
- **Design exercises power before anyone speaks.** Someone chooses the invitees, opening question, boundaries, process, duration and harvest. A live facilitator can refrain from steering answers and still shape what is possible. Corrigan's practical pre-design questions are: What is fixed and what is genuinely open? Who can say no? What can participants influence? What happens to what they produce? What can the convenor actually change or relinquish? A “co-create” invitation with an undisclosed sponsor veto can misrepresent the available agency.
- **Structure / interactions / design is a diagnostic, not a conformance model.** Corrigan's triangle locates existing authority, histories, policies and resources (**structure**); participants' relationships, resistance, interpretation and self-organization (**interactions**); and intentional constraints introduced by those empowered to convene (**design**). The companion labels these contextual, distributed and privileged power. This helps interrogate a particular gathering; it does not replace OFL's method essentials, interaction profile, runtime conventions and outcome evaluation layers. In his [reply to Howard Mason](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622442), Corrigan says internal hosts are also enmeshed in the system and that hosts *participate in*, rather than “host,” emergence.
- **Methods have bounded applicability.** A method changes the affordances for interaction; none is intrinsically good independent of purpose and context. Ask what it makes possible, what it makes harder, and whether it is useful *here*. The appropriate next move might be a structured meeting, a circle, an Open Space, or no meeting at all: observe the situation first. A registry describing how to run a method should not imply that using it was the correct choice.
- **Judgment is a practice, not a checklist.** Corrigan wants practitioners to notice before interpreting, see their own power, intervene without attachment, observe the response, and know when less facilitation is needed. He presents the Four Fold Practice and Chaordic Stepping Stones as aids to attention rather than prescriptive guarantees. His companion describes doing complexity-informed narrative capture and sense-making with people in the system *before larger-scale work* to inform the intervention design; that is his practice, not a universal precondition for all OFL methods.

## The outcome and evidence boundary

Separate at least three claims, each with different evidence and timescales: **an immediate participant benefit** (a useful experience, clearer difference, a possibility voiced); **work the group can own and use after the gathering** (a contested or revised harvest, an action someone can carry); and **later change to structures, resources, institutions or relationships beyond the gathering**. Positive session feedback supports neither the second nor the third by itself. Corrigan's “contact with context” asks what actually survives when people return to their constraints; it is not a promise that every gathering must transform a system. In the [companion comments](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622474), Ruth Picker notes that a valuable one-off experience can be worthwhile even without structural change. Avoid downgrading that real but narrower benefit.

**OFL application, proposed not adopted:** the conformance model's outcome layer belongs to the practice and has no agreed criteria. Practitioner review should specify which of the above claims a particular method aspires to, whose account counts, the relevant follow-up period and what evidence is unavailable from a transcript. A question about sponsor authority or use of a harvest may be a **precondition or design-risk check**, not a post-session outcome criterion. A method-essential turn check remains a different kind of evidence from either. These distinctions complement the [Zubizarreta-Ada extraction](2026-09-30-zubizarreta-group-facilitation-ai-alignment.md): co-creation inside a session and durable capacity or system change must not be conflated.

## Practitioner discussion in the comments

- [Corrigan to jpmort](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-dave-a-letter-on-hosting-complexity-and-practice/#comment-622372): an intuitive practitioner is not invisible while making consequential choices; ask others who saw the room differently, notice where an instinct led, and learn from it. This sharpens the accountability point rather than rejecting intuition.
- [Mark McKergow](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-dave-a-letter-on-hosting-complexity-and-practice/#comment-622373) rejects the claim that “leading from behind” removes influence. His comment is corroborating practitioner opinion, not evidence about a particular session.
- [Howard Mason](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622440) asks how the account changes when hosts themselves inhabit the system and keep acting in it; Corrigan's [reply](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622442) says their designer power still warrants accounting.
- [Ruth Picker](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622474) distinguishes the value of a one-off hosted experience from evidence of organizational change; she also asks whether work can connect to existing structures and processes.
- [Thomas Herrmann](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622494) points out that pre-work, follow-up and even report forms shape the process. Corrigan [agrees](https://www.chriscorrigan.com/parkinglot/dear-mates-a-letter-to-my-art-of-hosting-community/#comment-622495) that these phases need the same attention to structure, interactions and design.

## Questions to take to method stewards

- Who decides the invitation, question, participation boundaries and harvest format? Can participants see and contest consequential framing or synthesis?
- Which decision rights and vetoes are actually open, and how are they disclosed before asking people to co-create?
- When is this method appropriate, and what observation or non-meeting alternative should precede its selection?
- What is a valuable *session* outcome for this method, what later use is plausible, and who has the ability and responsibility to check either? Do not presume a system-level effect.
